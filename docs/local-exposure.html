Color

Local exposure

Normal auto exposure picks one brightness for the whole frame. In a scene with a dark room and a bright window that means either the room is black or the window is blown out. Local exposure fixes that by adjusting the brightness in different parts of the image separately, so dark areas get brighter and bright areas get darker while the overall exposure stays the same.

Unreal already has local exposure, and HLSLens keeps it working. The engine's own local exposure settings (Highlight Contrast, Shadow Contrast, Detail Strength and so on) are moved into a Local Exposure category on the volume, and HLSLens adds a choice of method.

Local exposure is applied before bloom and lens flares, so they react to the locally adjusted image.

Quality

Local Exposure Quality picks the method.

Option What it is
Cross Bilateral 2D The default. HLSLens' own method.
Bilateral Grid The same approach the engine uses. Pick it if you want local exposure to behave exactly like in a normal Post Process Volume.

Local exposure and lens flares

Lens flares and streaks see the image after local exposure. That keeps them consistent with what's on screen, but it can also make flares from very bright lights a bit weaker.
